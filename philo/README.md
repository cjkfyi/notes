# philo — video source archive

Notes vault for philosophy of physics, mind, and ethics: what the sciences imply about reality,
and what they do not.

Fifth topic area. Same layout and conventions as `../cybernet/`, `../health/`, `../neuroai/`,
`../bio/`.

## Why a separate area

`cybernet/` and `bio/` both contain metaphysical claims, but they arrive attached to a control theory
and a developmental biology respectively. This area is for sources whose **primary object is the
argument** — where the physics or biology is the material and the conclusion is philosophical.

The consequence for evidence standards is specific: **an argument can be invalid while every premise
in it is true.** So the grading here separates *premise accuracy* from *inferential validity*, and
marks each conclusion as valid / invalid / proves-too-much / unearned.

## Conventions specific to this area

- **Grade the premises and the inference separately.** A physicist can be entirely right about
  quantum mechanics and wrong about what it entails.
- **Name the position.** Sources frequently arrive at a well-developed philosophical position without
  naming it, which lets them skip its known objections. Find the name; find the objection.
- **Watch for "proves too much."** An analogy that would establish more than intended is the most
  common failure in popular physics writing. If it works equally well against the author's own
  conclusion, it is not doing the work claimed.
- **Distinguish "no description is privileged" from "there is no level of dependence."** These are
  different claims; only the first is usually supported. See `IR-20261026-ROVELLI-RELATIONAL-REALITY`
  §4.5.2.
- **Unfalsifiable is not the same as wrong.** A framework can be the best available orientation and
  still not be an empirical hypothesis. Say which it is.

## Convention added: access the primary source

The DMT report is the first in the archive where the underlying paper was **located and read**, and it
changed the assessment:

> **The video's title says "scientists say they can prove DMT entities are real." The paper's abstract
> says, in terms, *"Rather than asserting the reality of DMT entities…"***

**The body of the video is accurate and the title is not.** That discrepancy is invisible without the
primary source, and it is the single most useful thing the report produced.

Two conventions follow:

- **Retrieve the primary literature where it is cited and reachable.** It is the cheapest form of
  verification available and it can invert the headline.
- **Log what was retrieved and what was not** (Appendix H of the report). Three of four cited papers
  were paywalled. **Verification is bounded by access, not diligence** — which means this area will
  systematically over-represent claims whose literature happens to be open, and a source citing
  paywalled work is *harder* to check than one citing nothing.

## The three incompatible ontologies

The archive now holds three metaphysical frameworks, each derived from a different science, each
presented as a consequence of it, and no two of which can be simultaneously true.

| | **Levin** (`../bio/`) | **Rovelli** | **Hoffman** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fundamental | Patterns in a Platonic space | Relations; no intrinsic natures | **Conscious agents** |
| Mind | A pattern a body interfaces with | Not fundamental | **Fundamental** |
| Matter | An interface for a pattern | Relational appearance | **What consciousness projects** |
| From | Developmental biology | Quantum mechanics | **Perceptual psychology** |

**Pairwise, all three conflict.** A Platonic pattern existing prior to instantiation is what Rovelli's
relationalism forbids; a pattern does not experience, so Levin's fundamenta are not Hoffman's; conscious
agents have intrinsic natures, which Rovelli denies. **At most one can hold.**

**And on the archive's own discriminator — what does the framework predict that the observation does
not already contain? — the most metaphysically extravagant of the three is the only one with a
falsifiable positive prediction attached to a concrete protocol.** That is an uncomfortable result and
it is the correct one.

## The incompatibility this area was created to hold

The archive now contains **two directly opposed metaphysical systems**, each derived from a different
science, each presented as a consequence rather than a choice:

| | Levin (`../bio/`) | Rovelli (here) |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental | Patterns in a Platonic space | Relations, no intrinsic natures |
| Direction | Mind → matter (matter is an interface) | Relations all the way down |
| Foundation | **Yes, non-physical** | **No, and the question is empty** |
| Derived from | Developmental biology | Quantum mechanics |

**They cannot both be true.** Levin requires a realm of patterns existing prior to instantiation;
Rovelli's position forbids exactly that kind of intrinsic nature.

Neither is falsifiable as stated. But the archive's discriminator applies — *what does the framework
predict that the observation does not already contain?* — and on that test Levin's framework has the
**positive, checkable** prediction (novel interfaces produce unpredicted competencies), while
Rovelli's surplus content is a claim that certain questions have no answer. That is an uncomfortable
result for the more rigorous-sounding position, and it is the correct one.

**This deserves its own synthesis report.** One section in the Rovelli report is not enough; the
comparison should be worked properly, with the objections to each taken seriously. Flagged as the
highest-value outstanding task in the archive.
