# DMT Entities and the Conditional Argument

**Source** — "Scientists Say They Can Prove DMT Entities Are Real", `@Mushies`, 19:32.
Transcript: `transcript.md`.

A careful exposition of a preprint. This is the first source in the archive whose central claim could be
checked against the primary literature, and the check changed the assessment: **the video's title says the
paper proves something, and the paper explicitly declines to.**

> "Rather than asserting the reality of DMT entities, we draw on the phenomenology…" — the paper's abstract

The body of the video says the same thing: *"It doesn't prove that DMT entities are real. Not even close."*
So the packaging is wrong and the content is right, which matters because most viewers never get past a title.

## The paper

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Authors | Andrew Robert Gallimore, Niffe Hermansson, Donald D. Hoffman |
| Title | *Traces of the Other – Are DMT Entities Real? DMT Phenomenology in the Framework of Conscious Realism* |
| Venue | PsyArXiv (OSF Preprints), DOI 10.31234/osf.io/8qvgy_v2 |
| Dates | Submitted 26 May 2026; published 27 May 2026 |
| Status | **Preprint — not peer reviewed; zero citations** |

It is part of a named joint programme: **Noonautics** (Gallimore) provides the extended-state DMT protocol and
the phenomenological observation; the **Trace Institute** (Hoffman) provides the conscious-agent mathematics.
The programme's own description states its aim as "the first rigorous, empirically testable model… with clear
predictions that can falsify — or lend credence to — the hypothesis." That framing is honest and one sentence
in the video would have closed the gap between its title and its content.

## Conscious realism

Hoffman's position: consciousness is fundamental, and what we experience as matter is how interactions within
consciousness appear through a perceptual interface. This is a real, formally developed position — conscious
agents modelled as Markovian dynamics with experience, decision and action components, composed into
networks — not a podcast invention.

It is worth distinguishing from its neighbours: panpsychism holds that experience is a property of fundamental
*physical* entities, which conscious realism denies; idealism holds that only minds exist, whereas conscious
realism is structural and pluralist; mysterianism defers the hard problem, whereas conscious realism offers a
dissolution.

**The interface theory.** A blue folder icon is not a small blue folder inside the computer; it is part of an
interface that hides the underlying processes so the user can act effectively. The associated formal result —
that under a standard evolutionary model, veridical perception is strictly dominated by interface perception
for fitness — is published (Hoffman, Singh & Prakash, 2015). **It is a theorem about a model, not an empirical
finding**, and the model's assumptions do real work.

The evolutionary argument is correct in form and is the strongest part of the framework: natural selection
rewards behaviour that increases fitness, not perception of objective truth. What it does not establish is
that the *un*perceived is populated with agents — only that perception is selective. **The gap between
"perception is an interface" and "there are entities behind the interface" is where the hypothesis lives, and
it is an inference, not a result.**

## The qualia kernel

The paper's technical core, and the video's largest omission. From the abstract: representational limits on
the perceptible agent network are "partly determined by representational limits set by the qualia kernel that
governs transitions between experiential states."

```
A perceptual interface is characterised by a kernel over experience space.
The kernel determines which transitions between experiential states are reachable.
DMT perturbs the kernel → the reachable region expands → previously unreachable
   regions of experience space become representable.
```

Without the kernel, "DMT expands the interface" is a metaphor. With it, it is a **reachability claim** — and
reachability claims are the kind that can be given mathematical content. **The video explains the destination
and omits the vehicle**, which is why its account of *how* the interface is limited is vaguer than the paper's.

## The trace

You do not perceive another agent directly; you perceive the effect its actions have on the part of reality
your interface can represent. The radio analogy: thousands of signals pass through the room, but a receiver
tuned to a narrow band renders almost none of them.

This is the paper's central conceptual move and it solves a real problem — **how an imperceptible entity could
nonetheless be perceptible.** The answer is that you never perceive it; you perceive its shadow on the
representable surface. That is mathematically respectable (the standard structure of a projection) and is why
the hypothesis is not simply "DMT opens a portal."

It is also the framework's escape hatch. Because a trace is defined as a distorted projection into a
lower-dimensional space, *any* experience can be described as a trace of *something*. The paper's own
criterion for distinguishing a trace from a hallucination is that traces exhibit **"structured, stable, and
causally efficacious dynamics."** Those three words are doing enormous work and none of them is operationalised
in the video.

| Criterion | Question the video does not answer |
|---|---|
| **Structured** | Structured according to what grammar? Judged by whom? |
| **Stable** | Across subjects? Across sessions? Across doses? Over what timescale? |
| **Causally efficacious** | Causing what, measured how? |

Without operational definitions the discriminator collapses into the observer's judgement that the experience
felt structured — which is precisely the evidence the video's own skeptical chapter says is insufficient.

**Flatland.** A two-dimensional creature cannot represent a sphere; it sees a dot that becomes a circle, grows,
shrinks and vanishes. Correct, standard (Abbott, 1884), and used correctly: **an experience of impossibility is
exactly what a lower-dimensional receiver would report when a higher-dimensional object passed through it.**
That is the strongest intuition pump available for the hypothesis, and it is honestly labelled as an analogy.

## The logical form

| Step | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Reality consists of interacting conscious agents | **Framework assumption** |
| P2 | Perception is a species-specific interface over a thin slice | **Supported by a formal model, not measurement** |
| P3 | The slice is bounded by a qualia kernel | **Definitional within the framework** |
| P4 | DMT perturbs the kernel, expanding reachability | **Hypothesis** |
| C | Some DMT experiences may be traces of imperceptible agents | **Valid conditional conclusion** |

The argument is valid and its premises are not established. That is what a hypothesis looks like; the defect
would be presenting C as though P1–P4 were settled, and neither the paper nor the video does that.

The one place the framework risks circularity is P3. If the kernel is defined as "whatever limits what can be
experienced," then P4 ("DMT changes what can be experienced") is true by definition and carries no
information. **The framework escapes this only if the kernel is given independent mathematical content** —
which is what the paper attempts and what the video does not explain.

An audit of what is testable:

| Premise | Testable? | How |
|---|---|---|
| P1 — agents are fundamental | **No** | No measurement distinguishes it from its negation |
| P2 — interface, not veridical | **Partly** | Illusions, fitness-beats-truth models; not decisively |
| P3 — a kernel bounds it | **Only via predictions** | If the kernel has content, it predicts specific inaccessibilities |
| P4 — DMT perturbs the kernel | **Yes** | The proposed experiments |
| C — traces of agents | **Only indirectly** | Via P4, and only for information transfer |

**The only empirically tractable part of the framework is P4, and P4 does not entail P1.** The entire
experimental programme tests a downstream consequence of an untestable premise — which is not fatal, since
much of physics works this way, but it means the metaphysics is insulated from the experiments.

## The experiments

**Design A: the hidden random variable.** A computer in another room selects between two states. Neither the
participant nor the interacting researcher knows which. Can the entity report it?

This is well-formed and genuinely falsifiable: the target is a binary variable with a known prior; the
participant has no sensory access; the interacting researcher is blinded, removing the obvious leakage path;
the statistic is a simple binomial; and **it can fail.**

**Design B: cross-participant transfer.** Two isolated participants; can one deposit information and the other
retrieve it in a later session? Stronger and much harder, because it tests a shared external structure rather
than a single participant's access — a better match to the hypothesis of traces from a common agent network,
and the design most vulnerable to the hazards below.

**The problem with the test is that it is asymmetric:**

```
Positive result  ⟹  anomalous information transfer under DMT
                 ⇏  conscious realism, a qualia kernel, or an agent

Null result      ⟹  the traces were not renderable at this dose / in this subject
                 ⇏  conscious realism is false
```

**It can embarrass the framework and cannot confirm it.** That is a real epistemic advance over unfalsifiable
metaphysics — but it is not "prove these entities are real," and it is weaker than the video's closing
"mind-blowing, paradigm-shifting, revolutionary."

This is not a criticism of the paper, which is explicit that it is not asserting reality and whose programme
describes its aim as producing predictions that can falsify the hypothesis. **A falsification test for an
unfalsifiable-sounding claim is exactly the right contribution.** The criticism is of the framing.

What a genuinely confirming result would require: replication across laboratories (single-site psi-adjacent
results have a poor history); pre-registration, since the hypothesis space is large; preregistered stopping
rules, or optional stopping inflates significance; independent blinding of the analyst, not just the
interacting researcher; adversarial collaboration, with a skeptic co-designing the protocol; and a theory of
the mechanism, without which any positive result is an anomaly rather than a confirmation.

The video names several of these and omits **pre-registration** and **adversarial collaboration**, which are
the two that matter most for a claim of this kind.

## Methodological hazards

The video names sensory leakage, statistical error, researcher bias and fraud. Four further hazards are
specific to this design:

| # | Hazard | Why serious here |
|---|---|---|
| H-1 | **The participant is not a reliable observer** | A breakthrough DMT state degrades working memory, language and time perception. The report of the hidden variable must survive that degradation, and the reporting window is the state itself |
| H-2 | **Demand characteristics are enormous** | Participants know the hypothesis. Under a state that heightens suggestibility, a confident and wrong report is likely — and so is a correct report from subtle cueing |
| H-3 | **The researcher is also altered** | Blinding to the answer is not blinding to the expectation |
| H-4 | **DMTX is an experimental protocol with its own risk profile** | Extended intravenous infusion over hours is not the same intervention as inhaled DMT; safety data are limited |

**H-1 is the deepest and is not fixable by design.** Any test requiring a subject to report a specific fact
from inside a state that impairs reporting has a measurement problem before it has an inference problem. The
mitigation is to make the response channel as simple as possible — a button press, a spoken colour word — and
the timing must be within the state. The design description does not specify the channel, and the channel is
where the experiment will succeed or fail.

## What the video does well

Worth listing, because the source is unusual:

- It states that the paper does not prove the entities are real, **against its own title**.
- It devotes a full chapter to the skeptical case, in its own words: agency-detection bias, dream characters,
  unconscious knowledge, and the general unreliability of phenomenology as evidence.
- It distinguishes **skepticism from dismissal**, which is the correct distinction and rarely made.
- It names the neurological alternatives accurately and without caricature.
- It identifies falsifiability as the paper's real contribution — correct, that *is* the contribution.
- It notes that a **null result would also be informative**, which most popular treatments would not.
- It cites the primary literature with resolvable links, uniquely in this archive.

The best passage: *"Hallucination is a description. It isn't necessarily a complete explanation."* That is
philosophically correct — calling an experience a hallucination classifies it, it does not explain it. And the
follow-up is genuinely good: even the "boring" explanation would be remarkable, because the brain would then
contain machinery for generating autonomous-seeming minds that convince the experiencer.

The dream argument is the strongest skeptical point: you can dream about another person who says something
that genuinely surprises you, but your brain generated both sides of the conversation. **Unpredictability
alone does not prove external agency.**

## Corrections

- **The title.** "Scientists Say They Can Prove DMT Entities Are Real" is contradicted by the paper's own
  abstract and by the video's own skeptical chapter. The defensible title is "Researchers Propose a Test for
  Whether DMT Entities Are Real."
- **Author names.** The transcript renders them "Gallammore," "Galamore," "Gammore" and "Nifa Hermes." Correct:
  **Andrew Robert Gallimore**, **Niffe Hermansson**, **Donald D. Hoffman**. The first author is corrupted most
  often and is the programme's lead, so a reader trying to follow the work would search the wrong name.
- **"The empty entities might be hallucinations"** → **"the DMT entities."** Trivial, but it occurs in the
  video's thesis sentence.
- **"At the plank scale"** → Planck scale.
- **The qualia kernel is absent from the transcript entirely.** ASR destroys the term that carries the content.

## Verification

Only the abstract was retrieved; the OSF page is JavaScript-rendered and returned no body text, and the DOI
record supplied the abstract. Three of the four cited references — the Davis et al. entity survey, and two
Barker papers on endogenous DMT — are behind paywalls and were not read.

Three things the links were expected to settle and did not: the kernel's formal definition, whether the
experiments are pre-registered, and the actual phenomenology frequencies — needed to assess whether the
framework explains a robust phenomenon or a memorable one.

That is worth recording as a general finding: **the archive's ability to verify sources is bounded by access,
not diligence**, and a source citing paywalled literature is harder to check than one citing nothing.
